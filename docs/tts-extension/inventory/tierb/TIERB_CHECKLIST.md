Outcome meanings:

- **composes**: every part is a Tier A entry used in its Tier A meaning (VoQS: a Tier A mark applied over a braced stretch; V = engine default)
- **composes-via-alias**: a new letter/mark that a source equates with a Tier A composition; realise through the alias (see composition.ipa_equivalent; approximate=true where the equation is loose)
- **needs-new**: a new letter, a new mark, a new base (F, W, C, L, J...), or Tier A parts with a new meaning (status tierA-reinterpreted)
- **prosodic-control**: pauses, loudness, tempo, braces, degree numerals: realisable as prosody controls, not segment sounds
- **notation-only**: transcription conventions for indeterminacy or non-speech: nothing to say

Inside **needs-new** (132): 98 add a mark (or modifier letter, or base such as F/W/C/L/J) that is not in Tier A, 24 need a new letter, 1 is a convention needing a rule (reiteration), and 9 are written only with Tier A characters (plus a VoQS base letter) but mean something the Tier A meaning does not give (pre-aspiration, long aspiration, bunched r, larynx and jaw settings, laryngo-pharyngealized voice). 5 records are marked approximate. Acoustic correlates: 89 literature, 21 recalled-unverified; 128 records have none yet (no source opened for them).

## How counted

- **One record per symbol the chart prints**, voiceless and voiced members of a cell separately (as the Tier A checklist does), one record per example on the "other sounds" lines, one per diacritic or voicing row, one per convention (pause sizes, loudness letters, tempo words, the braces, each wildcard). The extIPA charts write "etc." after the release examples, and the literature allows any diacritic to be put in parentheses (Wikipedia, citing Ball et al. 2018); those open families are not expanded: only what is printed is counted.
- **extIPA is the union of three issues**: 2015 (the IPA site's `extIPA_2016.pdf`, "revised to 2015"), 2021 (the IPA site's `extIPA_2021.pdf`) and 2025 (ICPLA's current chart). `on_charts` says which issue prints each record. The 2015 text layer uses a custom font and private-use code points, so many 2015 grid symbols are `unclear` (the row and column labels are readable; the glyph is not). The JIPA article describing the 2015 revision (Ball, Howard & Miller 2018) could not be opened here: its PDF link returned the paywalled HTML page.
- **VoQS is the union of two issues**: VOQSchart_2016_X199 (ICPLA's current file; the 2016 revision of Ball, Esling & Dickson 2018) and VOQSchart_2015v4 (still linked on icpla.info as "updated 2016"). 2015v4 has two items 2016 dropped (whispery creak C̣, tight whisper W͈); 2016 adds ten falsetto/harsh/creaky combinations and a separate larynx-height heading.
- **Ids** are `B:` plus the code points (NFD) joined by `+`. A mark whose position carries the meaning gets `#pre`/`#post`; the loudness letters `#dyn`; the timed pause `#timed`; VoQS items written with the same characters as an extIPA item `#voqs`; tempo words `B:term:<word>`; `/` separates alternatives.
- **Composition** lists the Tier A ids (`IPA_CHECKLIST.json`) of every part that is a Tier A entry, and the other parts. A VoQS setting whose modifier is a Tier A mark in its Tier A meaning counts as `composes` (status `tierA-span`: the mark applied over the braced stretch); the braces are one `prosodic-control` record per chart and must be built once for both. V (modal voice) is the engine default.
- **Excluded**: the IPA consonant chart the extIPA chart refers to (Tier A); the up-arrow for egressive airflow and the isolated arrows for inhalation/exhalation (described in the literature, on none of the charts opened); Wikipedia's grid cells for laminal t̻ d̻ n̻ ɬ̻ ɮ̻ l̻ and its partial-denasal row (on none of the charts opened); the June 2015 pre-approval extIPA chart on icpla.info (superseded); superscript letters other than the printed release examples; circling of arbitrary letters (counted once, as the 'probably' oval).
- **2015 to 2021**: 2021 adds post-creak ◌˷, long aspiration ◌ʰʰ / ◌ʰ𐞁, the timed pause, median fricated release (tᶿ kˣ), wildcards for obstruent, liquid, glide, nasal, plosive, rhotic, sibilant, tone, nasal vowel, click and syllable, and an oval for 'probably'; drops Ⓥ; renames interdental aspiration to bidental aspiration; writes the buccal trill ↀ͡r̪͆ (2015: ↀ͡r̼); replaces most private-use glyphs with Unicode 14 code points (palatal and velar lateral fricatives and the voiced snort stay private-use); the notice changes from "© ICPLA 2015" to CC BY-SA.
- **2021 to 2025** (the 2024 changes in Ball 2024, of which only the metadata was opened; confirmed by the 2025 chart and Wikipedia): nareal fricative / nasal escape U+034B becomes U+033E; velopharyngeal friction U+034C becomes superscript feng U+10790; denasal and partial denasal merge into U+034A meaning partially denasal; the median-fricated-release line is gone; wildcards are written capital letter + U+20DD; 𝼆 𝼄 𝼀̬ use real code points.
- **Conflicts with Tier A** (decide before mapping): U+2193 is a Tier A alias for downstep but means ingressive airflow in extIPA and VoQS; U+A71E (superscript ¡, percussive release) is a Tier A alias for upstep; VoQS pharyngealized is written with U+02C1 by Wikipedia and U+02E4 in Tier A; VoQS "breathy voice" V̤ and "whispery voice" Ṿ follow Catford, so IPA breathy (Tier A ̤) corresponds to VoQS whispery.
