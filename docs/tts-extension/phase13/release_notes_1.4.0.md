OpenEVV SAPI5 1.4.0: **every language read by eSpeak NG now speaks with the sounds of the IPA master table.** This release also brings everything of 1.3.0, which was built but never published: every symbol of the IPA chart in the engine, every language as a data pack, Belgian Dutch, and two engine defects fixed.

**Please read this first.** Nobody who made this can judge most of these 146 languages by ear, and Claude, the AI that did the work, cannot hear at all. Every sound was measured, and every language was put through a speech recogniser, but many sounds are hypotheses waiting for a native speaker. Some languages may sound odd. No language is called authentic. If you speak one, please tell us: [Language review issue form](https://github.com/joshknnd1982/OpenEvv-sapi5/issues/new?template=language-review.yml).

## Installing

Download **OpenEVV-SAPI5-Setup-1.4.0.exe** and run it. You can run it with your screen reader running: nothing is closed and Windows does not need to restart. It installs over earlier versions and keeps your settings and languages. A program that was already running when you installed, such as your screen reader, keeps speaking as before until you close it and start it again. Windows may show "Windows protected your PC" for a new unsigned download: choose More info, then Run anyway.

SHA-256 of the installer: `821329171a28de7ff657131217c54068462344e8e804d67374abe7c8df0c663c`

## What's new in 1.4.0

Every language read by eSpeak NG now speaks with the sounds of the IPA master table. Nobody who worked on this can judge most of these languages by ear, and Claude, which did the work, cannot hear at all: every statement below rests on a measurement, many of the new sounds are hypotheses waiting for a native speaker's review, and no language is called authentic.

- **All 146 languages, dialects and accents read by eSpeak NG sound different.** In 1.3.0 every symbol of the IPA chart got a measured sound, but the languages still spoke with the sounds an older generator had written for them. Now each language's pack names its sounds as IPA and they are made from the master table when the pack is built (`realization = "master"`): a sound the module speaking the language already has is said as the module's own; one it lacks comes from the table; a language's own published measurements (stop timing, vowel formants) are used where a study gives them, each naming its source. A sound fixed once in the table is fixed for every language that has it. Of the sounds in the 146 packs, 6 stay on their 1.3.0 sound because the table cannot read eSpeak NG's IPA for them (French `#cFR`) and 12 so that two different phonemes do not come out alike (in Mandarin, Cantonese, Burmese, Marathi, Gujarati and Vietnamese); `docs/tts-extension/IPA_SWITCH_REPORT.md` lists each with its reason.
- **What it does to how well they are understood**, measured with a speech recogniser (Whisper large-v3, eight test sentences a language) against 1.3.0: over the 96 languages it can score, the median character error changed by -0.007; 52 are understood better, 44 worse. **Harder to understand by more than 0.10:** Cantonese, Welsh, Malay, Sindhi, Greek, Burmese and Faroese (Cantonese, Burmese and Sindhi were already near or above 1.0 in 1.3.0; in Malay, Greek and Welsh, Phase 12 found sentences that begin with a vowel heard as the recogniser's filler phrases). Eight languages vary from run to run: Cantonese, Sindhi, Welsh, Mandarin, Sinhala, Malayalam, Punjabi and Tamil gave a different figure in two runs of the same build (Cantonese 1.52 and 2.10), so Sindhi is on this list in one run and not in the other. Understood better by more than 0.02: 30, among them Ukrainian, Finnish, Russian, Swedish, Bulgarian and Belgian Dutch. Every language's figures are in `IPA_SWITCH_REPORT.md`.
- **Levels** (`docs/tts-extension/LANGUAGE_STATUS.md`): of the 156 languages, 1 at level 1 (Quechua, whose ejectives fail the engine check as in 1.3.0), 152 at level 2, 3 at level 4 (US English, New York English, Portuguese), none at 3 or 5. **Brazilian Portuguese dropped from level 4 to level 2**: one vowel measures 33 Hz outside its published range, though it is understood better than in 1.3.0. Mandarin, in both its spellings, rose from 1 to 2.
- **A fault found and fixed before release.** The installer build's check of every language pack found that in 65 languages a diphthong said with the module's own two-part phone (as in English *eye*, German *ei*) made the engine lose its place and put later words' sounds on the wrong vowels. Those diphthongs are now a vowel gliding to the next; the check passes for all 146, and the figures above were measured after the fix.
- **One language back to its 1.3.0 sounds:** set `realization = "legacy"` under `[engine]` in `packs/<tag>/pack.toml` and rebuild its folder with `python engine\packs\build_packs.py build languages <tag>`; the pack keeps its old sound lines for this.
- **Native speakers:** a new issue form, "Language review", asks how a language sounds to you; a correction can also be a pull request to the language's `pack.toml` or a review packet's filled `feedback.toml`. Review packets are ready for Welsh, Icelandic, southern Vietnamese, Cantonese, Finnish, Ukrainian, Malay, Tatar, Greek and Burmese, besides Scottish English and Belgian Dutch. A named native speaker's signed review raises a language to level 5 and changes nothing else by itself.
- Not changed: the ten languages the engine speaks itself, the engine's modules, the eSpeak NG front-end and its data, the SAPI interface, the settings, OpenEVV Configuration and the installer's choice of languages.
- Includes 1.3.0, below, which was not released on its own: the whole IPA chart in the engine, every language as a pack, Belgian Dutch, and two fixed engine defects.

## Also in this release: 1.3.0 (never published on its own)

The TTS extension: every symbol of the official IPA chart has a defined, measured sound in the engine; every language is a data pack; one new dialect; two defects of the engine fixed. Nobody who worked on this can judge most of these languages by ear, and Claude, which did the work, cannot hear at all: every statement below rests on a measurement, and no language is called authentic until a native speaker has signed its review.

- **New dialect: Dutch (Southern Standard, Flanders)**, tag `nl-be`, eight voices, beside Dutch. Eleven of its vowels are moved to the values published for Flemish men (Adank, van Hout and Smits 2004); its consonants, melody and vocabulary are Dutch's. It is at level 2 (the engine says what it is told). Said plainly: on the same eight test sentences the speech recogniser understands it less well than Dutch (character error 0.293 against 0.199), so it waits for a Flemish speaker's review. 156 languages in all.
- **What sounds different in the languages already here.** The seven modules that speak the languages read by eSpeak NG (146 now) are rebuilt with two fixes. (1) A voiceless sound after a stop, at the end of a word or in a cluster, no longer takes the voice the stop switched on for a following vowel (Russian "кот.", Hindi "आप.", New York English "cat." end in silence, not a hum). (2) Mandarin's neutral tone is 3 dB softer, as its table always said. And a voiceless l or w (as in Welsh *ll*) no longer lends its voice to the s after it. Of the 29,237 measured cases of the regression, 1,136 in 143 languages changed: 1,133 of them a consonant at a word's end or before another consonant, and 3 Shavian English vowels where the English module itself turns t before them into t and sh; the other 28,101 are identical. The ten languages the engine speaks itself are unchanged.
- **The whole IPA chart.** All 175 symbols of the 2026 chart (consonants, clicks, implosives, ejectives, vowels, diacritics, suprasegmentals, tone letters and tone marks) have a sound: 67 the engine already had, 53 composed from a base sound and a mark, 55 created; none missing, none silently replaced, each rendered by the engine and measured. 186 of the 203 extIPA and VoQS symbols too (17 are notation only). For this the engine's accent layer gained keys (trills at their own rate, a click's second release, ejective bursts, places of articulation, partial voicing, fricated releases, strikes, diplophonia, jitter, monotone, silent articulation) and the eSpeak NG front-end reads IPA marks, tone letters and extIPA braces and composes them on their sounds. These are tools for whoever writes a language: no language shipped in 1.3.0 uses the new keys yet, text is read as before, and speaking IPA through SAPI is a later addition. `docs/SOUNDS.md` lists every key; `docs/tts-extension/CREATED_SOUNDS.md` every created sound and what its values rest on (many are estimates awaiting a phonetician's or native speaker's check).
- **Every language is a pack**, `packs/<tag>/pack.toml`, from which the files in `languages\` are built; a dialect or accent `extends` its parent and holds only its differences, each with a source. `ADDING_A_LANGUAGE.md` is the guide, and `engine\doctor.py` checks everything in one command.
- **Each language has a level**, earned by measurement: of the 156, 3 at level 1 (draft), 149 at level 2 (engine-verified), 4 at level 4 (a speech recogniser understands its test sentences: US English, New York English, Portuguese, Brazilian Portuguese), none at level 3 or 5. `docs/tts-extension/LANGUAGE_STATUS.md` has every language.
- The About text of OpenEVV Configuration and the log name the engine `openevv 7148737+tts-ext`: the seven rebuilt modules are openevv 7148737 with this project's accent layer.
- Not changed: the SAPI interface, the settings, OpenEVV Configuration's pages, the installer's choice of languages (Belgian Dutch is one more box in it).
