## Descriptive phonetics sources for the acoustic correlates

116 sources, listed one by one with URL, licence, whether the source was actually opened, and what it was used for, in **`inventory/IPA_REFERENCES.md`** (generated from `inventory/research/*.json`). 112 were opened and read in this phase; 4 were not opened and are named only because an opened source quotes them. Two of those matter: the vowel formant values of Catford (2001) and of Peterson & Barney (1952) are in 25 vowel correlates **as quoted on an opened page** (Wikipedia's "Formant" article and a Princeton course page), and the checklist marks each one "as quoted there, not opened". Read the originals before any of those numbers becomes a target. Each correlate in the checklist names the sources it came from; a correlate with no opened source is tagged `recalled-unverified` and must be verified before any value is taken from it.

### The helper's notes

1. **Scottish English, best measurable source: CMU ARCTIC `awb`.** It is one male speaker, 74 MB, under a permissive CMU licence, and already has (automatic, US-phoneset) phone labels. Individual WAV and LAB files can be fetched from the unpacked tree without the tarball. For more than one speaker, use **OpenSLR 83 Scottish** (CC BY-SA 4.0, 620 MB male with 11 speakers, 351 MB female with 6, direct download, self-identified accent, no aligner output). VCTK has 19 Scottish speakers **with towns** but comes in an 11.7 GB zip.
2. **Southern American English: only VCTK p301 (NC), p308 (AL) and p310 (TN), plus p345 (FL), carry state-level region under an open licence that allows redistribution (CC BY 4.0).** The cost is the 11.7 GB zip. The host advertises HTTP ranges, so a remote-zip reader may fetch only those speakers (untested). The Common Voice "Southern American English" subset is only 32 MB and CC0, but it needs an MDC login and terms (no re-hosting, so not in the git tree; a no-voice-cloning clause; self-declared accents; two speakers make up 78% of it).
3. Both VCTK and CMU ARCTIC speakers read **scripted** text. The VCTK American speakers were recorded in Edinburgh and aged 18–33, so their accents may have shifted; mark them `measured` with that caveat.
4. **Literature for checking direction:** Clopper et al. 2005 (Southern back-vowel fronting, /æ/ fronting in Southern males, /ɛ ʌ/ longer; figures only, z-normalised, no Hz table). Scobbie et al. 1999 (SVLR durations in ms from figures). Hillenbrand et al. 1995 (Michigan; full numeric F1–F4 tables, a General American baseline).
5. **Licence differences to record:** PHOIBLE's site says CC BY-SA 3.0 but its repository data says CC BY 4.0. IDEA and the Speech Accent Archive (BY-NC-SA) cannot go in the tree. UCLA is CC BY-NC 2.0. CORAAL is BY-NC-SA 4.0.
6. **Glottocodes:** Southern American English = `sout3302` (parent `sout3300` Southeast American English). Glottolog has no Scottish Standard English languoid; `scot1243` is Scots (a separate language).
7. **Aligner:** MFA needs conda on Windows, and none is installed. The pip-only option with an open licence is the Apache-2.0 `facebook/wav2vec2-lv-60-espeak-cv-ft` CTC model plus our own Viterbi alignment. The MMS-based options are CC-BY-NC, and torchaudio's `forced_align` was removed in 2.9.
